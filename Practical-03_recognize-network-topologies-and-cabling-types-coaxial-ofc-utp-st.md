AIM : Recognize Network Topologies and Cabling Types (Coaxial, OFC, UTP/STP)

Network Topology

A network topology is the physical or logical arrangement of computers and other devices in a
network. It defines how devices are connected and communicate with each other.

Types of Network Topologies:

    •   Bus Topology: All devices are connected to a single backbone cable.




    •   Star Topology: All devices are connected to a central hub or switch.




    •   Ring Topology: Devices are connected in a circular loop.




    •   Mesh Topology: Every device is connected to every other device.

•   Tree Topology: A hierarchical combination of bus and star topologies.




Cabling Types

1. Coaxial Cable

A Coaxial Cable has a central copper wire, insulation, a metallic shield, and an outer cover. The
shield protects the signal from interference.

Uses:
    • Used for Cable TV, broadband internet, and CCTV cameras.
Example:
   • The cable connected to a set-top box or many CCTV cameras is a Coaxial Cable.




2. UTP (Unshielded Twisted Pair) Cable

A UTP Cable contains twisted copper wires without shielding. It is the most commonly used cable
in computer networks because it is low-cost and easy to install.

Uses:
    • Used in home, office, and college LANs to connect computers, switches, and routers.
Example:
   • The LAN cable connecting a computer to a Wi-Fi router is usually a UTP cable.

3. STP (Shielded Twisted Pair) Cable

An STP Cable is similar to a UTP cable but has an extra metallic shield to reduce electrical
interference.

Uses:
   • Used in factories, hospitals, and industrial areas where electrical noise is high.
Example:
   • A factory network with heavy machinery often uses STP cables.




4. Optical Fiber Cable (OFC)

An Optical Fiber Cable (OFC) transmits data using light through thin glass or plastic fibers. It
provides very high speed and supports long-distance communication.

Uses:
    • Used for Fiber internet, data centres, and telecommunication networks.
Example:
   • Jio Fiber and Airtel Fiber internet connections use Optical Fiber Cables.
